About

A map made of what people remember.

Most maps of Iran show roads, borders, and administrative units. This one shows what happened to people, and exactly where.

What counts as a narrative

A narrative is one account, told by one person, tied to two things that never move: an exact place and a period of time. A street corner in Tabriz in the spring of 1979. A schoolyard in Ahvaz across four years in the 1980s. A single afternoon at a bus stop.

Narratives are not written free-form. Everyone answers the same set of questions, which is what makes very different accounts readable side by side — and what stops the map from turning into a wall of unrelated text.

How the place and the time are recorded

  • Place. A pin, set to about a metre of precision, plus the name the contributor uses for it. The province is worked out from the coordinates, so filtering by province needs nothing extra from the contributor.
  • Time. A start and an end, with the precision the contributor actually has — to the year, the month, or the day. The map shows the period in both the Gregorian and the Solar Hijri calendar.

How submissions are reviewed

Nothing reaches the public map on its own. Every submission goes into a private queue, where a moderator reads it in full and either publishes it, sends it back, or declines it. The moderator can correct a typo or nudge a misplaced pin before publishing; the narrative itself stays as it was written.

Contact details are optional, are only used to ask a contributor a question before publishing, and are never shown on the map. A contributor who leaves the name field blank appears as “Anonymous”.

Languages

Answers can be written in English or Persian. Persian and Arabic text is detected and rendered right-to-left automatically, in both the reading panel and the list.

Where the map comes from

The outline of Iran and its 31 provinces is drawn from geoBoundaries (CC BY 4.0), bundled with the site so the map loads without calling out to anyone. The optional “Street detail” layer is the only thing that fetches third-party tiles, from OpenStreetMap.
